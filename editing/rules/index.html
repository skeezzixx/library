    II · Editing the scene¶

    Editing dialogue¶

    • Cut to the listener when the reaction is the story.
    • Stay on the speaker when the line changes the speaker.
    • Keep the line: screen-left looks right, screen-right looks left, in every shot of the exchange (continuity editing).
    • Roll early and hold late: the frames before and after the line are where the cuts live.
    • Establish the geography before you break it.
    • Never go tighter than the moment has earned.
    • Go wide on a physical event, a power shift, or a new presence.
    • The longer you don't cut, the more the first cut is worth.
    • Cut on the thought, not the line.
    • Lead the sound when the listener matters; trail it when the speaker does.
    • When in doubt, cut on the blink.
    • First cuts are too long — tighten the air before you tighten the shots.
    • Keep the pause that is the scene; cut the pauses that are only recording.
    • Steal the performance where it lives — sound from one take, the face from another.
    • Continuity of emotion beats continuity of props.
    • Choose the take for the scene, not the take for the line.
    • Don't metronome — vary shot length; ABAB reads as tennis.
    • Leave a reaction before it finishes when the next line is the answer.
    • The master is home — when the audience is lost, go back to the wide.
    • Re-establish whenever the geography changes.
    • Music enters on the turn, never under the setup.

    Deep dive: the generated dialogue scene¶

    • Record the voice first; the waveform is the shot's duration (animation).
    • Two-shot establishes, singles carry, reaction cutaways punctuate (TV animation).
    • Never require two independently moving characters in one generation (AI video).
    • Default every line to an off-mouth staging; the front-facing, lip-synced close-up is the rationed exception (limited animation, AI video).
    • Review every silent listening shot for mouth motion — a moving mouth is not evidence of sync (WAN 2.2).
    • Hold shots for 8–15 seconds; never live under 4 (children's TV, ages 5–8).
    • A held pose is a moving hold, never a freeze — never give the model two identical frames (animation, AI video).
    • Cut when the framing size changes; move the camera only to keep a spatial relationship on screen (TV storyboarding).
    • Neutral is the child's eye height; low and high angles are spent as beats (children's TV).
    • Spend the generations where the face is (naturalistic kids' TV).
    • Music is punctuation, not wallpaper (children's TV).
