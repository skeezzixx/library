    Anne Teresa De Keersmaeker¶

    Born Mechelen, Belgium, 11 June 1960. Trained Mudra, Maurice Béjart's school in Brussels (1978–80); Tisch School of the Arts, New York (1981). Company Rosas, founded 1983; resident at La Monnaie opera house (1992–2007); founded the school P.A.R.T.S. (Performing Arts Research and Training Studios, Brussels, 1995), the most influential contemporary dance school in Europe. Key collaborators Thierry De Mey and Peter Vermeersch (composers; De Mey also her filmmaker), Steve Reich's music (never the man — the works to his scores are hers alone), the ensemble Ictus (resident musicians), Ann Veronica Janssens and Michel François (visual artists), Dries Van Noten (costumes), Bojana Cvejić (dramaturg and co-author of her books), Boris Charmatz (duets), and the Rosas dancers across forty years.

    Contributions¶

    Music made visible, structurally. De Keersmaeker's work is the choreomusicology chapter's central contemporary case: choreography derived from the architecture of the score — its phrases, canons, phase relationships, geometric proportions — and made visible as floor pattern and ensemble structure. Fase: Four Movements to the Music of Steve Reich (1982) — Piano Phase, Come Out, Violin Phase, Clapping Music — phases two dancers as the music phases two tapes; Drumming (1998) and Rain (2001, Music for 18 Musicians) derive their spirals, circles, and pentagrams from the scores' structure so rigorously that her books (A Choreographer's Score, with Cvejić, 2012–) are part musical analysis, part geometry. The Bach works — Toccata (1993), Zeitung (2008), Partita 2 (2013, with Charmatz), Mitten wir im Leben sind (2017, the cello suites), the Goldberg Variations solo (2020), the Brandenburgs (2018) — continue the method on a different grammar. She is the composing chapter's case of process form on two channels, and the gamut chapter's case of a small vocabulary exhaustively permuted.

    Rosas danst Rosas. The 1983 breakthrough — four women, chairs, repeated everyday gestures (the hand through the hair, the tug at the shirt), sections of violent repetition to De Mey and Vermeersch's score — made her famous and the repetitive, task-like, feminine-but-unsentimental vocabulary a European signature; Beyoncé's 2011 "Countdown" video lifted it and De Keersmaeker said so publicly.

    Geometry as composition. Circles, spirals, the golden section, pentagons, the Fibonacci sequence drawn on the floor — her method (taught at P.A.R.T.S.) is to build a dance as a drawing in space and time, the dancers walking, running, and turning along the lines. Vortex Temporum (2013, Grisey, with Ictus playing among the dancers), Work/Travail/Arbeid (2015, a museum version of Vortex run for nine hours a day), En Atendant and Cesena (2010–11, ars subtilior, danced at dusk and dawn at Avignon) — the structure is the content.

    P.A.R.T.S. The school (with Cvejić's theory, Rosas's practice, and guest artists from the whole field) trained a generation of European choreographers (Salva Sanchis, Noé Soulier, Eleanor Bauer, Andros Zins-Browne, many more) and set the contemporary European standard.
