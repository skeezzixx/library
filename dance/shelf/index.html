    The shelf¶

    Part VIII · The shelf — overview · ← The pipeline, end to end · Isadora Duncan →

    The chapters of this subject are ordered by zoom — territory, language, notation, music, systems, staging, the synthetic dancer — and they carry the ideas. The people are here. Each page is an encyclopedia entry in the library's sense: a short factual header (dates, lineage, company), then an extensive account of what the choreographer contributed and descriptions of the major works, with links back into the chapters where the ideas are developed. The shelf browses as its own collection and is linked from everywhere the chapters name someone.

    The first wave, with the emphasis on modern and contemporary dance that the subject's core tier calls for.

    The founders and the first generation¶

    • Isadora Duncan — the barefoot revolutionary; the refusal as a career.
    • Vaslav Nijinsky and Bronislava Nijinska — the three shocks; Les Noces and Les Biches.
    • Martha Graham — contraction and release; the Greek cycle; the institution.
    • Doris Humphrey — fall and recovery; the group; The Art of Making Dances.
    • Katherine Dunham — dance anthropology as choreography; the technique.

    Ballet's twentieth century¶

    • George Balanchine — the plotless ballet; the music made visible.
    • Jerome Robbins — Broadway and ballet; the vernacular in the classical.

    Cunningham and after¶

    • Merce Cunningham — chance; independence; the decentralized stage; the computer.
    • Alvin Ailey — Revelations; the repertory company as institution.
    • Yvonne Rainer — Trio A; the "No Manifesto."
    • Steve Paxton — walking; Contact Improvisation.
    • Trisha Brown — the equipment pieces; accumulation; the released style.
    • Lucinda Childs — pattern permutation; Dance.
    • Twyla Tharp — structure; the crossover; pop scores.

    Europe and the contemporary¶

    • Pina Bausch — Tanztheater; pieces from questions; the difficult stage.
    • William Forsythe — ballet deconstructed; operations; choreographic objects.
    • Anne Teresa De Keersmaeker — the mathematician; Reich and Bach.
    • Ohad Naharin — Gaga; Batsheva; Decadance.
    • Wayne McGregor — the hyper-articulate body; Living Archive, AISOMA.
    • Crystal Pite — the crowd as organism; dance-theatre with text.
    • Akram Khan — kathak as contemporary language; Giselle, Xenos.
    • Hijikata Tatsumi and Kazuo Ohno — butoh — the dance of darkness; the white body; butoh-fu.

    Dance for the camera¶

    • Bob Fosse — the vocabulary from limitations; the number as camera.
    • Busby Berkeley — the overhead kaleidoscope; the camera as the only viewpoint.

    Second-wave candidates, as the chapters demand: Hofesh Shechter, Sharon Eyal, Sidi Larbi Cherkaoui, Rennie Harris, the Nicholas Brothers, Savion Glover, Michio Ito, La Argentina, Mary Wigman, José Limón, Pearl Primus.
