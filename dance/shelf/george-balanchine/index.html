    George Balanchine¶

    Part VIII · The shelf — page 6 of 24 · ← Katherine Dunham · Jerome Robbins →

    Born Georgi Balanchivadze, St Petersburg, 22 January 1904 · died New York, 30 April 1983. Trained the Imperial Theatre School, Petrograd (graduated 1921); the Petrograd Conservatory (piano and composition). Companies Diaghilev's Ballets Russes (ballet master, 1924–29); Les Ballets 1933; with Lincoln Kirstein the School of American Ballet (1934), American Ballet, Ballet Caravan, Ballet Society, and New York City Ballet (1948–83). Key collaborators Igor Stravinsky (from Apollo, 1928, to Agon, 1957, and the 1972 Stravinsky Festival), Lincoln Kirstein (the institution), Barbara Karinska (costumes), Jean Rosenthal and Ronald Bates (lighting), Jerome Robbins (co-ballet master at NYCB), and the dancers he made ballets on — Tanaquil Le Clercq, Maria Tallchief, Diana Adams, Allegra Kent, Suzanne Farrell, Patricia McBride, Edward Villella, Arthur Mitchell, and the generations of the "Balanchine ballerina."

    Contributions¶

    The plotless ballet. Balanchine did not invent dancing without a story — Fokine's Les Sylphides did — but he made it the central form of the century: ballets that are the music made visible, with no characters, no mime, no narrative, and the structure of the score as the structure of the dance. Serenade (1934), Concerto Barocco (1941), The Four Temperaments (1946), Symphony in C (1947), Agon (1957), Jewels (1967) — the choreomusicology chapter treats him as the master of structural visualization, the kind that renders the music's architecture rather than its surface, and the composing chapter describes the Stravinsky conversation in which the choreographer specified durations and the composer wrote to them.

    The American classical style. Speed, attack, energy, extension, off-balance and syncopation, the weight forward, the hips allowed to open, the line pushed past the Petipa norm — the "Balanchine technique" is a dialect of the classical lexicon built for a new body (long-legged, fast) and a new music; his company class and the School of American Ballet reproduce it, and his injunctions ("don't think, dear, just do," "what are you saving it for?") are its folklore. The "leotard ballets" (the costume history) stripped the costume to let it be seen.

    The institution. With Kirstein he built the model American company — school first ("but first, a school," 1934), then the company; a repertory of his own work performed in repertory; the Ford Foundation grants that seeded American ballet schools; the Balanchine Trust after his death, licensing the ballets with répétiteurs who set them — the mechanism by which his repertory became the most-performed in the world.

    Broadway and Hollywood. On Your Toes (1936, "Slaughter on Tenth Avenue"), Babes in Arms, Cabin in the Sky, the Goldwyn films — Balanchine made the musical's dance numbers integral, a line that runs to Robbins and Fosse.

    Major works¶

    • Apollon musagète (Apollo, 1928) and The Prodigal Son (1929) — the Ballets Russes works; Apollo the one he said taught him to eliminate.
    • Serenade (1934) — to Tchaikovsky, for his first American students; the fallen dancer, the hair coming down; the most-performed Balanchine ballet.
    • Concerto Barocco (1941), Ballet Imperial (1941), The Four Temperaments (1946), Symphony in C (1947), Orpheus (1948, Noguchi), La Valse (1951), Scotch Symphony (1952), The Nutcracker (1954 — the American Christmas institution), Western Symphony (1954), Allegro Brillante (1956), Divertimento No. 15 (1956), Square Dance (1957).
    • Agon (1957) — Stravinsky's twelve-tone score, twelve dancers, the pas de deux for Diana Adams and Arthur Mitchell; the century's central plotless ballet.
    • Stars and Stripes (1958), Episodes (1959, Webern), Liebeslieder Walzer (1960), A Midsummer Night's Dream (1962), Bugaku (1963), Movements for Piano and Orchestra (1963), Don Quixote (1965), Jewels (1967 — Emeralds, Rubies, Diamonds: the first full-evening plotless ballet), Who Cares? (1970, Gershwin), Stravinsky Violin Concerto and Duo Concertant and Symphony in Three Movements (1972, the Stravinsky Festival), Chaconne (1976), Vienna Waltzes (1977), Ballo della Regina (1978), Mozartiana (1981, the last major work).
