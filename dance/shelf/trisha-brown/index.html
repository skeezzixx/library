    Trisha Brown¶

    Part VIII · The shelf — page 12 of 24 · ← Steve Paxton · Lucinda Childs →

    Born Aberdeen, Washington, 25 November 1936 · died San Antonio, 18 March 2017. Trained Mills College (dance, 1958); Anna Halprin's summer workshop (1960); Robert Dunn's composition class in New York (1961–62). Company the Trisha Brown Dance Company, founded 1970 (still performing). Key collaborators Robert Rauschenberg (from Glacial Decoy, 1979), Laurie Anderson, Donald Judd, Nancy Graves, Fujiko Nakaya, the Judson group (a founding member), the Grand Union (1970–76), and her dancers (Stephen Petronio, Diane Madden, Vicky Shick, and others who carried the technique).

    Contributions¶

    The equipment pieces and the site. Brown's early work literally rotated the stage: Man Walking Down the Side of a Building (1970), Walking on the Wall (1971), Roof Piece (1971) — the props chapter's canonical site works, in which gravity was a sideways force, the city a set, and the viewer's assumptions about up and down the material.

    Accumulation and the rule-made dance. Accumulation (1971) — a gesture, then the gesture plus a second, then those plus a third, in a rotating solo of thirty minutes — is the cleanest rule-generated dance in the repertory, and the chance chapter treats it as the second-generation answer to Cunningham: not randomness but a visible procedure. Primary Accumulation (1972, lying down), Group Primary Accumulation, Accumulation with Talking plus Watermotor (1979 — the dance performed while telling two interleaved stories) extended it. Locus (1975) mapped the alphabet onto a cube around the dancer and spelled a text in movement; the Unstable Molecular Structure works (Opal Loop, 1980) built ensembles from individually rule-driven parts. Brown is the proof that a rule system can become an aesthetic rather than a demonstration.

    The released vocabulary. In the 1980s the rules produced a style: loose, sequential, initiated from anywhere, weight dropped and caught, the limbs flung and the spine liquid — the technique chapter's release lineage at its most articulate. Set and Reset (1983), with Rauschenberg's translucent set and costumes and Laurie Anderson's score, made on five instructions ("keep it simple," "play with visibility and invisibility," "stay on the edge," "act on instinct," "line up") is its masterpiece and the work companies worldwide learn her idiom through.

    The Valiant cycle and the opera house. Newark (Niweweorce) (1987, Donald Judd's colored drops), Astral Convertible (1989), Foray Forêt (1990, with a marching band passing outside the theatre), then M.O. (1995) to Bach's Musical Offering — the turn to classical music — and the operas (L'Orfeo, 1998, which she directed; Winterreise, 2002; Pygmalion) took the postmodern vocabulary into the institutions Judson had refused. She drew all her life; her drawings and "It's a Draw" performances (drawing on paper with her whole body) are in museum collections.

    Major works¶

    • Trillium (1962) — the first; Rulegame 5 (1964); Homemade (1966 — dancing with a projector strapped to her back, projecting a film of herself doing the dance).
    • Planes (1968), Man Walking Down the Side of a Building (1970), Floor of the Forest (1970 — a grid of ropes and clothes the dancers dress into), Walking on the Wall and Roof Piece (1971), Leaning Duets.
    • Accumulation (1971), Primary Accumulation (1972), Locus (1975), Line Up (1976), Watermotor (1978 — the solo that shows the style being born, famously filmed in slow motion by Babette Mangolte).
    • Glacial Decoy (1979 — Rauschenberg's first set for her; the dancers exiting and entering as if the stage were wider than the frame), Opal Loop (1980 — Nakaya's fog), Son of Gone Fishin' (1981), Set and Reset (1983), Lateral Pass (1985), Newark (1987), Astral Convertible (1989), Foray Forêt (1990), For M.G.: The Movie (1991), If you couldn't see me (1994 — a solo danced entirely with her back to the audience, Rauschenberg's music and costume).
    • M.O. (1995), Canto/Pianto (1997), L'Orfeo (1998), Five Part Weather Invention (1999), Rapture to Leon James (2000), Geometry of Quiet (2002), PRESENT TENSE (2003), O zlozony / O composite (2004, Paris Opéra), I love my robots (2007 — with Kenjiro Okazaki's moving poles), L'Amour au théâtre (2009), I'm going to toss my arms — if you catch them they're yours (2011 — the last, with industrial fans).

    Where she is in this subject¶

    The chance chapter (accumulation; rules as style); the props chapter (the site works); the technique chapter (release); the modern revolt (Judson and after); the costume history (Judson's pedestrian clothes; Rauschenberg).
