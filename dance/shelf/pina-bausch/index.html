    Pina Bausch¶

    Born Solingen, Germany, 27 July 1940 · died Wuppertal, 30 June 2009. Trained Folkwang School, Essen, under Kurt Jooss (1955–59); Juilliard, New York (1959–61: Antony Tudor, José Limón, Paul Taylor); danced with the Metropolitan Opera Ballet and with Jooss's Folkwang-Ballett. Company Tanztheater Wuppertal, director from 1973 until her death (the company continues). Key collaborators Rolf Borzik (designer, her partner, d. 1980), Peter Pabst (designer from 1980), Marion Cito (costumes from 1980), Matthias Burkert and Andreas Eisenschneider (music), Raimund Hoghe (dramaturg, 1980–90), and the dancers — Dominique Mercy, Malou Airaudo, Jo Ann Endicott, Lutz Förster, Nazareth Panadero, many others over decades — whose answers to her questions are the pieces.

    Contributions¶

    Tanztheater. Bausch made a form that is neither dance nor theatre and that every European stage has had to reckon with since: evening-length, non-narrative pieces assembled from fragments — repeated everyday gestures, social dances, spoken confessions, songs, games, cruelties, tendernesses — that accumulate into a portrait of how men and women treat one another. The modern-revolt chapter places her; the method is the thing to understand. From around 1978 she made pieces by asking the dancers questions ("what do you do when you're sad?" "show me a gesture of your mother's"), collecting the answers, and composing from them — so the material is the dancers' own and the authorship is hers; the dancers are credited as co-creators, and the works are remade, not restaged, on new casts. It is the craft chapter's tasking method at its most radical, and the chance chapter's frame-and-realization split with the frame being a question.

    Repetition as form. Her signature device — a gesture or scene repeated until it changes meaning, from charming to unbearable to funny to grief — is a structural principle: the piece is a set of motifs, returning with variation, and the audience's changing response to the same thing is the content. The gamut chapter's operator logic (repeat, vary, accumulate) is here turned on social behavior rather than movement.

    The difficult stage. With Borzik and then Pabst she made the set a terrain the dancers must negotiate — peat, water, turf, carnations, a collapsing wall, a rock — the props chapter's central case, and with Cito's gowns and suits (the costume history) she made costume a social fact the terrain ruins.

    Music as collage. Tango, blues, Purcell, pop, folk song, cut together, each fragment's relation to the movement chosen locally — the choreomusicology chapter's off-axis position.

    A lineage. The Folkwang line (Reinhild Hoffmann, Susanne Linke), the Belgian and French waves, Alain Platel, the whole European dance-theatre of the 1980s–2000s, and beyond dance the directors who learned from her (Pedro Almodóvar's Talk to Her opens with Café Müller; Wim Wenders's 3-D Pina, 2011, is the memorial).

    Major works¶

    • Fritz (1974) and the opera-dance works (Iphigenie auf Tauris, 1974; Orpheus und Eurydike, 1975) — the first Wuppertal years, still "dance."
    • The Rite of Spring (Le Sacre du printemps, 1975) — on a stage covered in peat, the Chosen One in a red dress, the ensemble's terror; the last work made as pure choreography before the method changed, and the most-performed.
    • The Seven Deadly Sins (1976, Brecht/Weill) — the turn toward theatre.
    • Blaubart (1977) — Bartók's opera on a tape recorder the protagonist stops and starts; a room of dead leaves. Komm tanz mit mir (1977); Renate wandert aus (1977).
    • Café Müller (1978) — a café of chairs; a sleepwalking woman (Bausch herself) in a slip; a man clearing chairs from her path; a couple repeatedly posed and dropped by a third. The emblem.
    • Kontakthof (1978) — a dance hall, the ensemble in evening clothes presenting themselves, courtship as humiliation and hope; remade with seniors over 65 (2000) and with teenagers (2008).
    • Arien (1979) — water across the stage, a hippopotamus; Keuschheitslegende (1979).
    • 1980 — Ein Stück von Pina Bausch (1980) — on turf, after Borzik's death; mourning, games, childhood.
    • Bandoneon (1980), Walzer (1982), Nelken (Carnations, 1982 — thousands of silk carnations, a man signing "The Man I Love," guard dogs).
    • Auf dem Gebirge hat man ein Geschrei gehört (1984), Two Cigarettes in the Dark (1985), Viktor (1986 — the first of the "city pieces," co-produced by and made about Rome), Ahnen (1987).
    • Palermo Palermo (1989) — the wall that collapses in the first minute; Tanzabend II (1991, Madrid), Das Stück mit dem Schiff (1993), Ein Trauerspiel (1994), Danzón (1995), Nur Du (1996, the American West), Der Fensterputzer (1997, Hong Kong), Masurca Fogo (1998, Lisbon), O Dido (1999, Rome), Wiesenland (2000, Budapest), Água (2001, Brazil), Für die Kinder von gestern, heute und morgen (2002), Nefés (2003, Istanbul), Ten Chi (2004, Japan), Rough Cut (2005, Korea), Vollmond (2006 — the rock and the water), Bamboo Blues (2007, India), Sweet Mambo (2008), and "…como el musguito en la piedra, ay si, si, si…" (2009, Chile) — the last, premiered weeks before her death.
