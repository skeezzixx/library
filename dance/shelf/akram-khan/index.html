    Akram Khan¶

    Part VIII · The shelf — page 21 of 24 · ← Crystal Pite · Hijikata and Ohno (butoh) →

    Born London (Wimbledon), 29 July 1974, to a Bangladeshi family. Trained kathak from age seven with Sri Pratap Pawar (Academy of Indian Dance); performed in Peter Brook's Mahabharata (1987–89) as a child; De Montfort University and the Northern School of Contemporary Dance; X-Group at P.A.R.T.S. (Brussels, 1999). Company Akram Khan Company, founded 2000 with producer Farooq Chaudhry. Key collaborators Nitin Sawhney, Jocelyn Pook, Vincenzo Lamagna and Ben Frost (composers); Michael Hulls (lighting); Tim Yip, Kimie Nakano (design); Sidi Larbi Cherkaoui (Zero Degrees), Sylvie Guillem (Sacred Monsters), Juliette Binoche (In-I), Israel Galván (Torobaka), the Shaolin monks' Cherkaoui cousin Sutra (lit by Hulls), Antony Gormley; the writers Karthika Naïr and Ruth Little (dramaturg).

    Contributions¶

    Kathak as contemporary language. Khan's body is a kathak body — the spins, the rhythmic footwork, the articulate hands, the speed and the stillness, the upright spine — and his contribution was to make that vocabulary a source for contemporary dance rather than a flavor applied to it. The early solos (Loose in Flight, Polaroid Feet, 2001; Ronin, 2003; Gnosis, 2010, with the kathak repertory performed straight in the first half) establish the grammar; the codified-vocabularies chapter and the South Asia atlas page treat kathak's recited rhythm, and Khan is its contemporary carrier. His Giselle (2016, English National Ballet — the Romantic ballet reset among migrant garment workers behind a wall, Lamagna's reworked Adam) is the most successful new full-length ballet of its decade and the proof that the language scales to a company.

    The duet as encounter. Zero Degrees (2005, with Cherkaoui; Gormley's casts of their bodies; Sawhney's score) — two men of migrant background telling a border story — made both men stars and set the form of the collaborative duet that defined a decade: Sacred Monsters (2006, Guillem), In-I (2008, Binoche), Torobaka (2014, Galván — kathak meets flamenco on the bull and the cow).

    Narrative and the world. Desh (2011 — a solo on Bangladesh, his father, the shipbreaking yards, Tim Yip's animated projections; Olivier Award), Until the Lions (2016 — the Mahabharata from Amba's view, in the round), Xenos (2018 — his last full-length solo, a colonial Indian soldier in the First World War), Outwitting the Devil (2019 — Gilgamesh), Jungle Book reimagined (2022), the London 2012 Olympic opening-ceremony section: Khan made the migrant, the colonial subject, and the mythic the matter of British dance at scale.

    Major works¶

    • Fix (2000), Rush (2000), Kaash (2002 — Anish Kapoor, Sawhney; the first ensemble work), Ronin (2003), ma (2004), Zero Degrees (2005), Sacred Monsters (2006), bahok (2008, with the National Ballet of China), In-I (2008), Gnosis (2010), Vertical Road (2010), Desh (2011), iTMOi (2013 — in the mind of igor, for the Rite centenary), Torobaka (2014), Kaash revival, Until the Lions (2016), Giselle (2016), Chotto Desh (2015, for children), Xenos (2018), Outwitting the Devil (2019), Creature (2021, ENB), Jungle Book reimagined (2022), Gigenis (2024 — a return to the classical Indian forms with an ensemble of masters).
